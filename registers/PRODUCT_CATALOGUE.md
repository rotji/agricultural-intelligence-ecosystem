# Agricultural Product and By-product Catalogue

**Catalogue policy:** Open-ended and continuously expanding.  
**Initial setup date:** 20 November 2026  
**Last reviewed:** 20 November 2026

## 1. Purpose

Maintain a structured, evidence-linked index of agricultural and agrifood products, inputs, commercial forms, processing stages, co-products, by-products, residues and emerging products.

The catalogue is an index, not a boundary on research. Any relevant product discovered outside the current catalogue can be investigated and proposed for addition. There is no fixed maximum number of entries.

## 2. Product hierarchy

Use the following structure where applicable:

**Product family → commodity/species/crop → variety or grade → commercial form / processing stage → by-product or co-product → SKU/specification.**

Examples of relationships to capture include:
- Cassava → fresh roots → peeled/chipped roots → starch/flour/garri → peels and wastewater.
- Rice → paddy → milled rice → broken rice/bran/husk/straw.
- Groundnut → pods/kernels → oil/roasted kernels → press cake/hulls.
- Poultry → live birds → meat/eggs → feathers/blood/litter/manure.

These are classification examples, not assertions about local availability or market demand.

## 4. Product record template

For each product or distinct commercial form, capture:
- Product ID: PRD-XXXX (stable; never reused).
- Canonical name and known aliases.
- Parent product/family ID.
- Product family.
- Commodity/species/crop and variety/grade.
- Commercial form and processing stage.
- Classification: raw product / input / processed product / co-product / by-product / residue / emerging product.
- Description and uses.
- Related products and transformation relationships.
- Relevant geography and seasonality.
- Quality/specification and standard where known.
- Units and normalisation details.
- Shelf life, storage and handling where relevant.
- Production/processing requirements where known.
- Market level and demand evidence.
- Sources and evidence IDs.
- Discovery date and last verified date.
- Record status: Proposed / Under classification / Evidence-backed / Monitored / Inactive.
- Attention level: Priority A / Priority B / Discovery queue.
- Confidence and unresolved questions.
- Update history.

Do not merge distinct grades, varieties or processed forms when their markets or technical characteristics differ.

## 6. Discovery and prioritisation rules

Every research cycle must:
1. Monitor existing catalogue entries where relevant signals are available.
2. Search for new products, forms, inputs, co-products, by-products and residues beyond the existing list.
3. Record newly discovered items as proposed entries with sources.
4. Investigate commercial significance before promoting an item to evidence-backed status.
5. Link related products to supply chains, producers, processors, buyers, solutions and opportunities.
6. Rotate attention so that less prominent product families are not perpetually neglected.

Priority A is for active investigation, Priority B for regular monitoring and Discovery queue for new or poorly understood entries. Priority is dynamic; the catalogue is not a list of items that must all be researched deeply every day.

## 8. Relationship to opportunities

A product discovery is not automatically a business opportunity. Create or link an OPP record only when there is a specific problem, demand signal, supply gap, commercial use, solution route or other opportunity hypothesis worth investigating. Keep product facts and opportunity hypotheses distinct.
