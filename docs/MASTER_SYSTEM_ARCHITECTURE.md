# Agricultural Ecosystem Intelligence and Opportunity Discovery System

## Master System Architecture and Governance Blueprint

**Version:** 0.5 working baseline, including the continuity amendment and selected capability-assessment safeguards  
**Date:** 20 November 2026  
**Status:** Approved working architecture; implementation remains subject to evidence and capability validation.

## 1. Mission

Create a continuously evolving intelligence and opportunity-discovery system that explores agriculture and the agrifood economy, identifies developments and unmet needs, connects evidence across geographies and value chains, evaluates existing solutions and participation routes, and supports practical decisions and execution.

The system should move from broad discovery to verified understanding, opportunity selection, practical action and recorded outcomes. It must remain understandable, portable, evidence-grounded and useful even if its creator, tools, vendors or technical implementation change.

### 2.1 Continuous Investigation and Opportunity Continuity

Every significant discovery must be traceable from initial identification through investigation, validation, pursuit, completion, rejection or deliberate suspension. Each active item must have a unique identifier, supporting evidence, current status, next action, dependencies and a review date or revisit condition. New evidence must be able to change priorities or reopen previous investigations. No valuable discovery should disappear merely because a new daily report has been produced.

Daily intelligence is cumulative work, not a series of disconnected reports. Persistent records are maintained in repository registers. Daily automation must only claim to have read or updated these records when access and the corresponding operation have actually succeeded.

## 3. Information classification

Classify information as:
- **Verified Current Reality:** established by reliable, current evidence.
- **Approved Direction:** a decision or direction accepted by the project owner.
- **Proposed Information:** a recommendation or design proposal not yet approved.
- **Unresolved Information:** open questions, evidence gaps and uncertainty.
- **Historical Information:** previously relevant facts, observations or decisions retained for traceability.

Separate observed facts, source-reported claims, analytical inferences, hypotheses and recommendations.

## 4. Scope

### 4.1 Geography

**Plateau State:** all 17 LGAs: Barkin Ladi, Bassa, Bokkos, Jos East, Jos North, Jos South, Kanam, Kanke, Langtang North, Langtang South, Mangu, Mikang, Pankshin, Qua'an Pan, Riyom, Shendam and Wase.

**Nigeria:** all 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory. Do not exclude less prominent states or concentrate indefinitely on a few locations.

**International:** relevant producers, buyers, exporters, importers, processors, commodity markets, suppliers, technology providers, institutions, policies and events. Trace credible causal pathways into Nigeria and Plateau State; state explicitly when no material pathway is established.

### 4.2 Open-ended agricultural product universe

The initial approved catalogue contained 60 items, with an intended expansion beyond 100. Neither 60 nor 100 is a ceiling or boundary. The catalogue is an evolving index and prioritisation aid.

Explore all relevant agricultural and agrifood products, commercial forms and value-chain inputs, including:

- Crops: grains, roots, tubers, pulses, oilseeds, fruits, vegetables, spices, herbs, nuts, tree crops, fibres, flowers, forage, pasture, seeds and planting materials.
- Livestock and animal products: poultry, dairy, eggs, meat, hides, skins, wool, manure, breeding stock, feed and animal-health inputs.
- Fisheries and aquaculture: fish, aquatic plants, fingerlings, fish feed and processing outputs.
- Other biological products: forestry, agroforestry, apiculture, mushrooms, snails and other commercially relevant products.
- Agricultural inputs and services: seed, fertiliser, soil amendments, crop protection, veterinary products, feed ingredients, irrigation, energy, machinery, tools, packaging, storage, finance, insurance and logistics.
- Processed and semi-processed products: flour, starch, oils, meals, cakes, flakes, chips, dried, frozen and canned products, beverages, extracts, fibres, bio-based materials and industrial ingredients.
- By-products, residues and circular-economy streams: peels, husks, bran, straw, stalks, shells, pomace, press cakes, bones, blood, feathers, whey, manure, wastewater and processing residues, including existing and potential uses.
- Emerging products: niche, indigenous, under-commercialised, substitute, export-oriented and new products discovered in research, patents, trade data, markets and informal value chains.

Use a hierarchical product model:

**Product family → commodity/species/crop → variety or grade → commercial form / processing stage → by-product or co-product → SKU/specification where known.**

Do not treat distinct forms, grades or processing stages as interchangeable. Distinguish raw materials, finished products, inputs, co-products and by-products. Capture units, quality, seasonality, shelf life, quantity, market level and processing requirements where available.

### 4.3 Product discovery and attention programme

Operate two complementary processes in every research cycle:

**A. Catalogue-wide monitoring:** review relevant price, production, supply, demand, trade, policy, weather, pest/disease, logistics, processing, investment and technology signals across the broad product universe.

**B. Open-ended discovery:** search for unlisted products, indigenous products, commercial forms, by-products, residues, industrial uses, emerging products, substitutes and new value chains. New discoveries become proposed catalogue additions with a classification and evidence reference.

Apply dynamic attention levels:

- **Priority A — Active investigation:** strongest current evidence, material change, actionable demand/opportunity or urgent risk. Specify the next verification or action.
- **Priority B — Regular monitoring:** meaningful commercial or strategic relevance without immediate action.
- **Discovery queue:** newly found or poorly understood products/forms requiring classification and initial evidence gathering.

Priority is dynamic and evidence-led. Explain why a product receives attention and what evidence could change its rank. Rotate research to avoid repeatedly overlooking less prominent products and locations. Do not imply every item receives deep investigation every day. Disclose research coverage limits.

Signals that may elevate priority include unusual price/availability movement, verified buyer demand or supply gap, import/export opportunity, local production potential, processing/value-addition potential, valuable by-product use, policy intervention, seasonal window, new buyer or technology, food-security relevance, cross-sector linkage and practical participation potential.

## 5. Twelve intelligence divisions

1. **Production and Agricultural Resources Intelligence:** production locations, land, water, climate, soils, seasons, yields, farming systems, producer capacity and constraints.
2. **Commodity and Product Intelligence:** product universe, varieties, grades, raw/processed forms, co-products, by-products, specifications and uses.
3. **Price and Market Intelligence:** farmgate, aggregation, wholesale, retail, processor, import/export and other relevant market levels.
4. **Supply Chain and Distribution Intelligence:** inputs, aggregation, storage, transport, cold chain, wholesale, retail, exports and losses.
5. **Processing and Industrial Intelligence:** processors, capacity, conversion, technology, by-product utilisation, industrial demand and value addition.
6. **Trade, Finance and Investment Intelligence:** local and international trade, buyers, procurement, financing, grants, insurance, investment and regulations.
7. **Global Agricultural and Agrifood Intelligence:** international production, supply, demand, prices, policies, events and transmission pathways to Nigeria.
8. **Innovation, Technology and Solutions Intelligence:** existing solutions, providers, research, patents, local inventions, maturity and deployment.
9. **Opportunities Discovery and Classification:** demand, supply gaps, business models, access routes, participation requirements, risks and capital levels.
10. **Creative and Unconventional Problem-Solving Intelligence:** cross-sector connections, resource recombination, reverse thinking, business-model shifts and assumption testing.
11. **Decision Intelligence:** evidence-weighted comparisons, scenarios, prioritisation, uncertainty and recommendations.
12. **Execution, Outcome and Learning Intelligence:** actions, ownership, dependencies, time/cost, outcomes, blockers, lessons and feedback.

## 6. Geography and entity registries

### 6.1 Agricultural Producer, Production and Infrastructure Registry (APPI)

The proposed APPI Registry should represent:
- Geographic entities: states, LGAs, wards, communities, villages and coordinates.
- Agricultural entities: farms, plantations, livestock farms, fish farms, hatcheries, cooperatives, producer groups and research farms.
- Infrastructure: irrigation, mechanisation, warehouses, cold rooms, markets, collection centres, processors and logistics facilities.
- Product relationships: products, varieties, production seasons, capacities, quantities, processing and sales destinations.

Capture dates and evidence for every record. Missing administrative detail must not prevent capturing reliable GPS or locality information. Absence of a record is not evidence that no agricultural activity exists.

### 6.2 Relationship mapping

Map where products originate, where they are aggregated, processed, stored, transformed and sold. Capture relationships only when evidence supports them; distinguish confirmed links from inferred or proposed links.

## 7. Evidence and source governance

Use four evidence levels:

- **L1 — Verified completed transactions:** completed trade with sufficient traceable supporting evidence.
- **L2 — Verified quotations:** quotations from identifiable sources, with date and terms.
- **L3 — Unverified claims:** advertisements, seller posts, informal quotations or claims not independently confirmed.
- **L4 — Qualitative signals:** discussions, reports, perceived shortages, emerging demand or other contextual indicators.

For market observations, capture as available:
- Product, form, grade and quality.
- Price, currency and unit.
- Market level and location.
- Quantity/volume.
- Date observed and source publication date.
- Delivery, packaging and payment terms.
- Source identity and verification status.

Never present an asking price as a completed transaction. Never treat stale data as current. Preserve conflicting observations. State when reliable current prices are unavailable.

Source records should capture provenance, dates, geography, confidence, licensing/usage constraints, freshness, coverage and known gaps.

## 8. Innovation, technology and solution intelligence

The unit of discovery is the **solution**, not just the company. Include startups, established providers, manufacturers, universities, research institutions, government programmes, NGOs, local inventors, patents, open-source projects and informal solutions.

Solution areas include seeds/genetics, soil/nutrients, crop protection, irrigation, machinery, precision agriculture, livestock, aquaculture, energy, storage, processing, logistics, markets, finance, insurance, software and AI. Search for transferable solutions outside agriculture too.

Solution maturity:
- S0: problem identified, no known solution.
- S1: research or concept.
- S2: prototype or laboratory demonstration.
- S3: field test or pilot.
- S4: commercially available.
- S5: demonstrated deployment with identifiable users.
- S6: scaled or established across markets.

Do not confuse vendor claims with independent deployment evidence.

## 9. Opportunity discovery and classification

Maintain a dynamic Opportunity Register. Each record should contain:
- Unique ID, title and discovery date.
- Originating evidence and source references.
- Problem/demand statement and affected users.
- Geography, product and value-chain stage.
- Existing solutions/providers and alternatives.
- Opportunity type and potential revenue mechanism.
- Demand evidence and confidence.
- Competition, risks and regulatory considerations.
- Capital level, skills and participation requirements.
- Status, priority, objective, next action, dependencies and review date.
- Update history and closure/revisit conditions.

Opportunity lifecycle: **Discovered → Investigating → Validated → Pursuing → Closed or Parked.**

Items may be reopened when new evidence warrants it. Use detailed status definitions in the continuity governance document.

Capital levels:
- Level 0: zero capital.
- Level 1: low capital.
- Level 2: medium capital.
- Level 3: high capital.
- Level 4: complex or strategic capital.

Zero capital does not mean zero effort, risk, skills or time. Distinguish opportunities accessible to an individual from those requiring an organisation, licence, assets or capital.

## 10. Creative and unconventional problem-solving

For significant problems, generate multiple distinct approaches. Explore cross-industry connections, analogy, reverse thinking, resource recombination, waste-to-value, unused capacity, business-model transformation and assumption breaking.

Treat unconventional ideas as hypotheses until demand, providers, costs, regulations, technical requirements and execution feasibility are investigated. Do not present novelty as validation.

## 11. Decision intelligence

Assess, as relevant:
- Demand and evidence strength.
- Market size and practical accessibility.
- Existing providers and competitive alternatives.
- Participation requirements, capital, skills and time.
- Revenue mechanism and economics.
- Execution dependencies and regulatory conditions.
- Commercial, operational, environmental and reputational risks.
- Scalability, strategic value and uncertainty.

State assumptions and confidence. Present trade-offs. The user retains final decision authority.

## 12. Execution, outcome and learning

Track selected actions against expected outcomes. Record action date, result, time/cost, blockers, evidence, lessons and adjustments. Feed outcomes back into discovery and prioritisation.

Distinguish:
- What was proposed.
- What was authorised.
- What was attempted.
- What was completed.
- What outcome was evidenced.

## 13. Shared conceptual data model

Potential entities include geographic_entities, agricultural_entities, agricultural_facilities, commodity_products, production_records, entity_product_relationships, market_observations, supply_chain_relationships, international_events, impact_assessments, intelligence_sources, evidence_records, problems, solutions, organizations, technologies, innovation_projects, solution_deployments, solution_problem_links, solution_product_links, solution_location_links, solution_organization_links, partnership_opportunities, solution_assessments, competitive_alternatives, opportunity_records, decision_records, action_records, investigation_records, execution_records and outcomes.

This is a conceptual model, not a claim that a database has already been implemented.

## 14. Intelligence cycle

**Discover → Capture evidence → Classify → Identify patterns and changes → Connect entities and value chains → Detect problems and opportunities → Generate approaches → Identify existing providers → Validate demand and feasibility → Compare and recommend → Execute → Record outcomes → Learn and reprioritise.**

The cycle is iterative. New evidence can send an item back to investigation.

## 15. Daily intelligence output

Use three layers:

1. **Five-minute front brief:** overall assessment; five key developments; three strongest opportunities; two things to watch; one practical action; one unconventional connection labelled as a hypothesis where unvalidated.
2. **Full intelligence report:** detailed evidence and findings across all twelve divisions, geography, products, markets, supply chains, processing, global pathways, providers, risks, opportunities and coverage gaps.
3. **Action checklist:** no more than three concise actions, the single most worthwhile next investigation and carry-forward items.

The front brief must remain concise. Full research remains available for deeper reading.

## 16. Daily continuity and review

Every daily run must check accessible prior briefs and persistent registers before creating new investigations. Carry forward active actions, blockers, dependencies and unresolved questions. Update statuses only when supported by evidence. Review daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly at the appropriate level.

A scheduled automation is not itself a persistent database. Repository records are the source of truth only when successfully read and committed. Report access failures transparently.

## 17. Solution selection and build discipline

Do not build when an established solution already addresses the need. First investigate existing providers and capabilities, partnership and referral routes, distribution and representation, integration and implementation, aggregation, coordination and managed services, local availability, access restrictions, licensing and economics.

Build only when a genuine evidenced gap remains and there is a concrete customer request, incentive or strategic reason. Reassess alternatives before committing resources.

## 18. Documentation governance and repository structure

The repository uses:
- docs/ for governing architecture and operating procedures.
- registers/ for current opportunities, actions, investigations and decisions.
- daily-briefs/ for dated, immutable report history.
- evidence/ for source and evidence provenance.

Keep navigation and summary information current. Preserve historical records and explain material changes. Do not delete knowledge simply because a decision or opportunity is no longer current.

## 19. Implementation status

This blueprint defines the intended system. It does not by itself implement a live commodity database, automatic data ingestion, a continuously updated producer registry, transaction verification or a market API. Implement capabilities incrementally, only after validating need, access, reliability, cost and maintenance requirements.


## 20. Capability-first assessment and solution decisions

This section adapts the relevant capability-first and technology-neutral guidance from the user's Universal Project Governance Standard v1.1 to this agricultural intelligence ecosystem. It does not adopt or reproduce the universal standard wholesale.

### 20.1 Assessment sequence

Before proposing or adopting a technology, service, operating process, infrastructure or new system, identify the required capability independently of the proposed solution. Apply this proportionate sequence:

1. **Requirement:** State the specific agricultural intelligence, production, market, verification, coordination or decision outcome required.
2. **Required capability:** Define what the ecosystem must be able to do to produce that outcome.
3. **Existing resources:** Identify suitable people, institutions, processes, data sources, platforms, providers, infrastructure and tools that already offer some or all of the capability.
4. **Sufficiency:** Determine whether existing capabilities are sufficient, partly sufficient or insufficient for the actual requirement.
5. **Specific gap:** If insufficient, explain exactly what remains unavailable or inadequate. Do not label a missing skill, data permission, operating relationship or access right as a software gap.
6. **Alternatives:** Consider using an existing resource, improving a process, reusing or configuring a platform, partnering with a provider, integrating services, licensing/procuring a capability or developing something new.
7. **Decision and rationale:** Record the proportionate decision and why it fits the evidence, constraints and expected outcome.
8. **Validation:** Define how the chosen approach will be tested against the original requirement.
9. **Reassessment trigger:** Identify what material change, failure, cost increase, scale requirement, policy change or new evidence would justify reviewing the decision.

For this ecosystem, examine existing government programmes, research institutions, producer organisations, private companies, commodity platforms, data providers, logistics operators, processors and technology vendors before proposing a new service or product. The suitable solution may be an introduction, implementation, distribution, partnership, data service or operating process rather than software.

### 20.2 Decision outcomes

Use these statuses when a material capability or solution assessment is made:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Not required | The project does not currently need the capability or technology. |
| Existing capability sufficient | Current resources reliably meet the requirement. |
| Reuse or configure existing solution | An available solution can meet the requirement with reasonable adaptation. |
| Integrate or combine existing solutions | Multiple existing capabilities can jointly deliver the outcome. |
| Capability gap identified | A specific required capability remains unmet. |
| Solution under evaluation | Alternatives are being examined; no selection has been made. |
| Adopted and validated | The selected approach has been implemented and tested against its intended requirement. |
| Deferred | The capability may matter later, but adoption is not currently justified. |
| Rejected | The option is unsuitable or does not provide sufficient value. |
| Development justified | Evidence supports creating a new component because a meaningful gap remains. |

A provider's advertised feature or a platform's existence does not establish that the capability is available to this project. Verify actual access, permissions, operating status, compatibility, cost and terms before treating it as a usable capability.

### 20.3 Proportionate use and reassessment

Apply this assessment at meaningful decision points: when a new capability is required, a repeated operational bottleneck appears, an existing provider or integration is being considered, a pilot fails, scale or risk changes, or a material change in cost, access, policy or performance occurs.

Do not turn routine tool use or every daily discovery into a formal assessment exercise. A short explanation that existing capabilities are sufficient is adequate for a low-risk decision. Use a more detailed assessment when the choice materially affects project direction, expenditure, legal or regulatory exposure, security, data access, infrastructure, long-term dependencies or maintenance.

Where a detailed record is warranted, capture the requirement, capability, existing resources, sufficiency, exact gap, alternatives, selected status and rationale, expected value and trade-offs, validation method, decision-maker/date and reassessment trigger. Link it to relevant evidence, opportunity or decision records.

The governing rule is: **identify the required capability, examine what already exists, and introduce additional complexity only when an evidenced requirement justifies it.**

## 21. Information, data and external-service safeguards

Apply proportionate safeguards when collecting, storing, analysing, sharing or publishing agricultural intelligence:

- Respect source terms, data licences, attribution requirements, API conditions, rate limits and restrictions on reuse or redistribution. Public accessibility alone does not mean unrestricted reuse.
- Distinguish public information from operational/internal, confidential or restricted information. Do not put credentials, passwords, private personal data, confidential business records or sensitive producer-level information in a public repository.
- Before connecting an external platform, API, connector, marketplace or AI service, verify that the required operation is supported and that account eligibility, authorisation, permissions, cost, usage limits, data handling, retention and security are acceptable.
- Collect and disclose only information necessary for the stated research purpose. Preserve provenance and avoid exposing private contact details or commercially sensitive information without an appropriate basis.
- Treat information supplied by third-party platforms and automated systems as claims requiring source and quality assessment, not as verified reality merely because it was returned by a tool.

These safeguards apply to producer and facility mapping, market observations, stakeholder contacts, platform integrations and future data-sharing arrangements. The depth of review should reflect the sensitivity and consequences of the information or connection.
